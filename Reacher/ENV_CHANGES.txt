================================================================================
REACHER ENVIRONMENT CHANGES -- THE FOUR PERTURBATION CELLS
================================================================================
Written 2026-08-31.  Describes the four cells run in results/traces100/
(4 cells x 5 arms x 100 seeds x 200 steps = 2,000 episodes).

Source of truth: env/reacher.py, test_reacher_default_head.py.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SHARED SETUP (identical across all four cells)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Base task     Reacher-v5 (MuJoCo).  2-link arm, 2 torque actuators, reach a
              randomly placed target.  Workspace is a disk of radius ~0.21 m.

Observation   OBS_DIM = 10.  Dims 8:10 are (fingertip - target) in (x, y) --
              the reward-carrying dims.  Action ACT_DIM = 2 (joint torques).

Reward        r = -||fingertip - target|| - ||a||^2   (distance + control cost).
              UNCHANGED by every perturbation below.  All four faults perturb
              the DYNAMICS only; the reward function is never touched.  For the
              saturation/rotation faults the control cost is charged on the
              COMMANDED action, not the perturbed torque, so the planner cannot
              be rewarded for the fault.

Episode       200 steps, NO TRUNCATION.  (Reacher-v5's native TimeLimit is 50
              steps; that is overridden.  200 is load-bearing -- an online
              adapter given only 50 steps sees 50 samples with which to learn a
              2-D force field.  The trace curves show arms do not separate until
              ~step 20-40, so a 50-step episode would miss most of the effect.)

Change onset  ts = 0 for every cell.  The fault is present from the first step;
              there is no clean pre-change phase within an episode.

Discount      gamma = 1.0  (NO DISCOUNT)
Planner       CEM + CVaR, horizon H = 15, J = 500 candidates, 8 CEM iterations,
              K = 3 posterior draws, alpha = 0.8.  Per real action this rolls
              out J*K = 1,500 imagined trajectories x 15 steps x 8 iters =
              180,000 imagined model-steps.
Seeds         1000..1099 (100 per cell).  Every arm runs every seed, so all
              comparisons against no_adapt are PAIRED on identical episodes.
Arms          nl1_forget_qv, nl1_noforget, no_adapt, forget_elbo, oracle
Surprise      per-dimension surprise score; surprise -> smooth -> inflate.

================================================================================
FAULT A -- NON-LINEAR WIND  (cells 1 and 2)
================================================================================
A static-per-episode, SPATIALLY-varying force field applied to the fingertip.

  F_x(x,y) = F_max * tanh( sin(2*k_s*x + C_x1) + sin(pi*k_s*y + C_x2) )
  F_y(x,y) = F_max * tanh( sin(2*k_s*y + C_y1) + sin(pi*k_s*x + C_y2) )

  - The four phase offsets C are drawn once at reset and HELD FIXED for the
    whole episode.  Within an episode the wind is therefore a fixed function of
    POSITION, not a time-varying disturbance.  This is deliberate: a fixed field
    is learnable by a state-conditioned adapter, which is what the head is asked
    to do.
  - The force is written into data.xfrc_applied at the body's CURRENT position
    once per MuJoCo SUBSTEP (frame_skip = 2), so it is closed-loop -- the arm
    feels the field it is currently moving through, not a constant push.
  - Applied to the fingertip body, so the disturbance is a pure end-effector
    force and the field the arm feels is exactly F(fingertip_x, fingertip_y).
  - F_max: 0 -> 500 at ts = 0.

  Note on F_max = 500: CLAUDE.md's default "wind intensity 0 -> 5" is INERT on
  Reacher.  At F=5 the wind shifts joint velocity ~0.007 rad/s per step against
  a model one-step RMSE of ~0.24 on those dims -- roughly 34x BELOW the model's
  own noise floor, and statistically indistinguishable from no wind.  Reacher's
  actuator gear is 200 N*m at ctrl=1, so a 5 N force on the 0.21 m lever is 0.5%
  of actuator authority.  The "intensity 5" convention is inherited from
  LunarLander, which is ~380x softer in the relevant units.  F=500 was chosen as
  the point where headroom is real but the ORACLE has not itself collapsed
  (F=1000 degrades the oracle, meaning the task breaks rather than the model).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CELL 1 -- WIND, k_s = 0.5      (F_max 0 -> 500 at ts 0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NATURE      Low spatial frequency.  Across the 0.21 m workspace the sine
            arguments sweep only a fraction of a radian, so the field is
            NEAR-UNIFORM -- effectively a large, smooth, position-dependent
            push with very long coherence length.

WHY IT      This is the LEARNABLE end of the frequency axis.  The field is
MATTERS     spatially coherent, so a qvel-restricted adapter can represent and
            cancel it.  It is also the regime with the LARGEST headroom.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CELL 2 -- WIND, k_s = 5        (F_max 0 -> 500 at ts 0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NATURE      Mid spatial frequency.  The field's wavelength becomes comparable
            to the arm's own excursions, so the fingertip crosses crests and
            troughs on its way to the target.

WHY IT      This is the HARDEST cell for everyone, not just for the adapter.
MATTERS     Headroom COLLAPSES to +16.66 -- the smallest of the four cells --
            because the disturbance degrades the ORACLE too (oracle -16.19,
            its worst of any cell) while no_adapt actually improves relative to
            k_s=0.5.  Less is available to win, so a smaller win is expected.

  FREQUENCY CONTEXT (from the 3-seed sweep, results/ks_sweep/):
  nl1_forget_qv's advantage is MONOTONIC in k_s: +13.9 / +12.3 / +1.9 / -3.3
  at k_s = 0.5 / 1 / 5 / 15.  It stops winning by k_s = 15.  The mirror-image
  arm lin1_noforget wins ONLY at k_s = 15 (+2.3) and loses everywhere else.
  So "which arm wins on wind" is not a fact about the method -- it is a fact
  about k_s.  k_s = 15 (the historical default, chosen because k_s=1
  "degenerates to a near-uniform push") is the single WORST frequency in the
  range for showing that adaptation works: the physical reading was right, but
  near-uniform is exactly what makes a fault learnable.

================================================================================
FAULT B -- ACTUATOR ROTATION  (cells 3 and 4)
================================================================================
A transmission-coupling fault: the commanded torque vector is ROTATED.

  tau = R(theta) @ a,     R(theta) = [[cos, -sin], [sin, cos]]

  - R is ORTHOGONAL, so ||tau|| == ||a|| EXACTLY.  The planner keeps its entire
    torque budget and cannot lose by "getting there slower" -- the whole
    performance gap is DIRECTION error.  This makes theta a single clean
    difficulty knob.
  - Physically: a cable- or tendon-driven arm has a motor->joint coupling
    matrix; re-tensioning or re-routing changes it.
  - Applied to the COMMANDED action on its way into the simulator, BEFORE any
    saturation (rotation is a property of the transmission).  The control cost
    is still charged on the commanded a, so the reward function is untouched.
  - Expected progress toward the target falls roughly as cos(theta).
  - Wind is DISABLED in these cells (--fmax 0).

  IMPORTANT FLAG HAZARD: the driver's --fmax DEFAULTS TO 500.  A rotation run
  that omits "--fmax 0" silently runs wind AND rotation simultaneously.  This
  has already invalidated at least one historical batch (results/mulqv_rot60.json
  carries fmax=500 alongside rot=60 and is excluded from all analysis).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CELL 3 -- ROTATION, theta = 90 deg     (applied at ts 0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NATURE      Torque rotated a quarter turn.  cos(90) = 0, so the commanded
            direction contributes ZERO expected progress toward the target:
            a full channel swap-and-flip.  Torque magnitude is unchanged.

WHY IT      Just past the crossover.  Below ~60 deg the fault is too small to be
MATTERS     worth modelling -- the frozen model's bias costs less than the
            adapter's added variance, and every adaptation arm LOSES.  90 deg is
            the first angle where adaptation clearly pays.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
CELL 4 -- ROTATION, theta = 150 deg    (applied at ts 0)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
NATURE      Torque very nearly REVERSED.  cos(150) = -0.866, so the commanded
            direction actively drives the fingertip AWAY from the target.
            Magnitude still unchanged.

WHY IT      The most severe fault in the whole study and the largest headroom
MATTERS     (+78.84).  Cleanest separation between arms; every seed agrees.

CROSS-CELL SUMMARY
================================================================================
                          headroom   nl1_forget_qv   nl1_noforget   forget_elbo
  wind  k_s = 0.5           +21.50    +8.32 (74%)     -2.33          -6.43
  wind  k_s = 5             +16.66    +2.60 (57%)     -0.74          -8.49
  rot   90 deg              +25.33    +7.35 (72%)     -2.01          -5.69
  rot   150 deg             +78.84   +52.19 (100%)    -4.05         +32.75
  (parenthesis = per-seed win rate against no_adapt out of 100)

THREE FINDINGS THAT HOLD ACROSS ALL FOUR CELLS

1. nl1_forget_qv is the ONLY arm that beats the frozen model in every cell.
   This overturns the earlier "all adaptation arms lose" conclusion, which was
   recorded at wind k_s=15 -- an operating point that turns out to be the worst
   available for both faults.

2. nl1_noforget (ELBO refit, NO inflation) is NEGATIVE in all four cells.
   Since nl1_forget_qv = ELBO + surprise-triggered inflation, the inflation is
   doing the work, not the refit.

3. forget_elbo INVERTS between the two fault types: second-best arm at rot 150
   (+32.75, 100/100 wins) and the WORST arm in both wind cells (-6.43, -8.49).
   Refitting the body's output layer helps against a clean action-space rotation
   and actively hurts against a spatial force field.

SEVERITY IS THE CONTROLLING VARIABLE.  In both faults the adapter's advantage
grows with fault size: rotation crosses from loss to win between 60 and 90 deg,
and wind's advantage decays as k_s rises.  The advantage is not a fixed property
of the method -- it is a function of where on the severity axis you sample.
